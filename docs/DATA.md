# `data/world.js` — the Burlington map dataset

Baked, real-world geometry for downtown Burlington, Vermont, centred on the
Church Street Marketplace. Generated by [`tools/build_world.py`](../tools/build_world.py)
from OpenStreetMap and USGS 3DEP. Nothing in this file is invented — every
number traces back to one of the two sources below.

```js
import { WORLD } from "../data/world.js";
```

- **Size:** 1,080,330 bytes (1.08 MB), one JSON object, one line per record.
- **Area:** lat 44.4735 → 44.4830, lon −73.2205 → −73.2065 — Church Street from
  the Unitarian Church at Pearl St south to Main St / City Hall, Battery St to
  the west, S. Union St to the east, King St to the south.

---

## 1. Coordinate convention (a contract — do not change it)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Origin** | OSM node `204493047`, the Church Street × College Street intersection, `44.4770964, −73.2125662` |
| **+x** | metres **EAST** of the origin |
| **+z** | metres **SOUTH** of the origin (so **north is −z**) |
| **+y** | metres of elevation **relative to the origin's ground level** (origin terrain y = 0) |
| **Projection** | equirectangular at the origin latitude: `x = (lon − lon₀)·111320·cos(lat₀)`, `z = (lat₀ − lat)·111320` |
| **Origin ground** | 63.44 m above sea level (USGS 3DEP) — stored as `WORLD.origin.elev_m` |

Church Street runs almost exactly north–south, so the mall is roughly the
`x ≈ 0` line and the game's "down the mall" direction is **+z**.

Going the other way:

```js
const M = 111320, mlon = M * Math.cos(WORLD.origin.lat * Math.PI / 180);
const lat = WORLD.origin.lat - z / M;
const lon = WORLD.origin.lon + x / mlon;
```

Coordinates are rounded to **0.1 m** and heights to **0.1 m**. `WORLD.origin.lat`
/ `.lon` are the one exception — they keep full precision, because 0.1° of
latitude is 11 km.

## 2. What is in `WORLD`

```
WORLD = {
  origin:       {lat, lon, elev_m, osm_node},
  bbox:         {minX, maxX, minZ, maxZ},        // local metres
  attribution:  ["© OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL)", "Elevation: USGS 3DEP"],
  terrain:      {x0, z0, step, cols, rows, heights:[...]},
  churchStreet: {centerline:[[x,z],...], crossings:{Pearl,Cherry,Bank,College,Main}},
  roads:        [{id, name, kind, lanes, oneway, width, surface, maxspeed, sidewalk, pts}],
  areas:        [{id, kind, name, pts, tags}],
  buildings:    [{id, name, pts, holes, h, hsrc, levels, type, colour,
                  material, roof, addr, onChurch, side, tags}],
  pois:         [{id, name, kind, x, z, building, tags}],
  props:        [{kind, x, z, tags}],
  lines:        [{kind, pts, tags}]
}
```

### `terrain`
Row-major grid of heights **relative to the origin's ground level**, rows
increasing in **+z** (southward), columns increasing in **+x** (eastward).

```js
const t = WORLD.terrain;                       // 257 cols × 246 rows @ 5 m
const y = t.heights[row * t.cols + col];        // metres, relative
const x = t.x0 + col * t.step, z = t.z0 + row * t.step;
```

`63,222` samples covering the whole bbox plus a 20 m margin. Bilinearly
resampled from a 400×400 float32 3DEP raster (≈4.3 m native). No-data cells are
filled by nearest neighbour; there were **none** in this build.

### `churchStreet`
The ordered centreline of the `highway=pedestrian` mall, **north → south**,
trimmed to the Pearl → Main span (29 points), plus the five named cross-street
intersections. Deliberately *not* Douglas-Peucker simplified — the street is
nearly straight, so any tolerance would collapse it to its two endpoints and
lose the per-block vertices.

| crossing | x | z |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pearl | −17.2 | −372.4 |
| Cherry | −11.3 | −245.7 |
| Bank | −5.6 | −122.8 |
| College | 0.0 | 0.0 |
| Main | 6.2 | 128.2 |

The mall is 500 m long. **`highway=pedestrian` between Pearl and Main means no
cars** (OSM tags delivery-only access, Mo–Sa 07:00–10:30).

### `buildings`
`pts` is the outer ring, **not closed** (do not repeat the first point).
`holes` is an array of inner rings, present only when the footprint has one —
**no building in this build has a hole**.

`h` is metres, and **`hsrc` says where it came from** — check it before
trusting a height:

| `hsrc` | meaning | count |
|---|---|---:|
| `"osm"` | surveyed `height` tag (largely the VCGI LiDAR import) | 1,088 |
| `"levels"` | `building:levels` × 3.4, +1 m for a commercial ground floor | 5 |
| `"default"` | nothing in OSM: 8 m if commercial/retail, else 7 m | 65 |

Coverage is unusually good here — 94 % of footprints have a real surveyed
height. The tallest are the Federal Building (44 m), the Co-Cathedral of Saint
Joseph and the Masonic Temple (40 m each).

`onChurch: true` marks the 52 footprints that come within 13 m of the mall
centreline between Pearl and Main; `side` is `'E'` (30) or `'W'` (22).

### `pois`
352 named features with a `shop` / `amenity` / `tourism` / `historic` /
`leisure` / `man_made` / `craft` / `office` tag. `kind` is `"<key>:<value>"`
(e.g. `"shop:clothes"`). `building` is the id of the building polygon that
contains the point, when there is one — that is the association the game uses
to hang a storefront sign on a facade.

### `props`
677 point items of street furniture — benches, bollards, bike parking,
crossings, kerb nodes, traffic signals, memorials, artwork, hydrants. `kind` is
again `"<key>:<value>"`.

### `lines`
67 polylines that are skateable edges: `steps` (26), `barrier:fence` (24),
`barrier:wall` (8), `man_made:pier` (4), `barrier:hedge` (3),
`barrier:retaining_wall` (1), `barrier:bollard` (1). `steps` records carry
`step_count` / `incline` where OSM has them. Lines that leave the area are cut
into pieces rather than truncated.

### `areas`
125 ground polygons: `amenity:parking` (46), `landuse:*` (51 across
residential / commercial / retail / construction / grass / …),
`highway:pedestrian` areas (6), `leisure:park` (4 — including **City Hall
Park**), plus Lake Champlain, Battery Park, the Elmwood Avenue Cemetery and
Champlain College.

## 3. Elevation sanity table

USGS 3DEP, metres. `abs` is above sea level; `rel y` is the value the game
sees. Cross-checked against 3DEP's `getSamples` point service at every row —
**maximum disagreement 0.33 m**, which is the expected bilinear-vs-native
difference.

| intersection | x (m E) | z (m S) | abs (m ASL) | rel y (m) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Church & Pearl | −17.2 | −372.4 | 69.5 | +6.0 |
| Church & Cherry | −11.3 | −245.7 | 68.0 | +4.6 |
| Church & Bank | −5.6 | −122.8 | 66.9 | +3.4 |
| Church & College | 0.0 | 0.0 | 63.4 | 0.0 |
| Church & Main | 6.2 | 128.2 | 59.9 | −3.6 |
| St Paul & Main | −114.2 | 135.0 | 56.7 | −6.8 |
| Battery & Main | −485.5 | 153.3 | 38.4 | −25.0 |
| S Winooski & Main | 125.2 | 122.7 | 58.5 | −4.9 |
| Pine & College | −244.0 | 12.8 | 56.4 | −7.0 |

**Reading the slope.** Church Street drops **9.6 m over its 500 m** from Pearl
to Main — a steady ~1.9 % grade *southward*, which is exactly the gentle roll
the mall has in real life. The east–west streets fall much harder toward the
lake: Main Street loses **21.5 m** from Church down to Battery (≈4.5 %), and
College Street loses 7 m from Church to Pine. Lake Champlain sits at 29.6 m in
this raster, the true lake surface, and the terrain maximum in the box is
94.2 m at the south-east corner where the land climbs toward S. Union St.

## 4. Sources, licences, and required attribution

**The game must display both lines below** wherever it credits its data.
They are also carried in `WORLD.attribution` so the code can render them
directly.

### OpenStreetMap — geometry, buildings, streets, POIs, furniture
- Fetched from the **Overpass API** (`overpass-api.de`, falling back to
  `overpass.kumi.systems` then `overpass.osm.ch`).
- Snapshot for this build: **2026-08-23**.
- Licence: **Open Database License (ODbL) v1.0** — <https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/>
- Required attribution: **`© OpenStreetMap contributors`**
- ODbL is share-alike *on the database*. Shipping this derived extract inside
  the game is fine as long as the credit is shown; if the extract itself is
  redistributed as data, it must stay under ODbL.
- Many Burlington building heights and footprints in OSM come from a **VCGI**
  (Vermont Center for Geographic Information) import — visible as the
  `ref:vcgi:esiteid` tag on 620 ways. That import is why heights are so well
  covered here — 1,088 of 1,158 footprints carry a surveyed `height`.

### USGS 3DEP — elevation
- `https://elevation.nationalmap.gov/arcgis/rest/services/3DEPElevation/ImageServer`,
  `exportImage` in EPSG:4326, `pixelType=F32`, 400×400.
- Licence: **public domain** (a work of the U.S. Government).
- Requested credit: **`Elevation: USGS 3DEP`**

### Not used
- **City of Burlington / Vermont Open Geodata building footprints** were *not*
  fetched separately. They were unnecessary: 1,088 of 1,158 buildings already
  carry a real `height`, and the VCGI import means OSM here *is* largely the
  state dataset. Adding a second source would have introduced a reconciliation
  problem for no gain.
- **Open-Elevation** and 3DEP `getSamples` are coded as fallbacks in
  `build_world.py` but were not needed — `exportImage` succeeded.

## 5. Re-running the build

```bash
python3 tools/build_world.py            # rebuild from tools/cache/ (offline, ~20 s)
python3 tools/build_world.py --refresh  # re-download OSM + elevation, then rebuild
python3 tools/build_world.py --refresh-osm
python3 tools/build_world.py --refresh-elev
```

**Python 3, standard library only.** No `pip install`, no virtualenv, no
numpy, no PIL. The GeoTIFF is parsed by a small `struct`-based reader in the
script that handles uncompressed float32 in both tiled and striped layouts.

Downloads are cached in `tools/cache/` (`osm.json` ≈ 3.8 MB, `elev.tif` ≈ 1.0 MB)
and are **not committed** — the script re-fetches them on demand. Rebuilding
from cache is deterministic; a `--refresh` picks up whatever OSM looks like
that day, so re-check the counts in `data/world-summary.txt` after one.

Both services are public and free; the script sends a descriptive `User-Agent`
and backs off exponentially on 429/5xx. Be polite — do not loop `--refresh`.

Outputs: `data/world.js` and `data/world-summary.txt` (counts per category, the
full Church Street POI list east side then west side, the sanity table, and the
gap list). The sanity table is also printed to stdout on every run.

### Tuning knobs (top of `build_world.py`)
`BBOX`, `TERRAIN_STEP` (5 m), `ELEV_IMAGE_SIZE` (400), `SIMPLIFY_TOL`
(Douglas-Peucker, 0.3 m), `ON_CHURCH_DIST` (13 m).

If the file ever grows past the 1.5 MB budget, raise `SIMPLIFY_TOL` or
`TERRAIN_STEP` first — the terrain grid is ~30 % of the file.

## 6. Known gaps — read this before dressing the scene

These are recorded automatically on every build and reprinted at the bottom of
`data/world-summary.txt`.

1. **OSM has ZERO `natural=tree` nodes in this bbox.** The Marketplace's double
   row of street trees does not exist in the data. It must be authored by hand.
2. **ZERO `highway=street_lamp`**, **ZERO `man_made=planter`**, **ZERO
   `amenity=fountain`.** Same story — the mall's lamp posts and concrete
   planters are not mapped.
3. **Only 13 `amenity=bench`** in the entire bounding box. The real mall has
   many times that. `props` alone will not dress Church Street; treat it as a
   skeleton, not a set-dressing list.
4. **65 of 1,158 buildings** have neither `height` nor `building:levels` and
   fall back to 7 m / 8 m. They carry `hsrc: "default"` — filter on that rather
   than guessing. Named ones include Butler Hall, Ethan Allen Engine Company
   No. 4, Stratos Condominiums and The Nest.
5. **`CityPlace Burlington` (`w614518204`) is baked at 8 m with
   `hsrc: "osm"`** — that is genuinely what OSM records for the footprint, not
   a fallback. It looks far too short for what that block is becoming, which is
   consistent with the site still being mapped mid-construction (see 7 below).
   Do not silently "fix" it; it is what the source says.
6. **41 of the 52 Church St mall buildings have no `name`.** Their storefronts
   are mapped as separate POI *nodes* instead, associated back to the building
   via `pois[].building`. Sign placement must go through the POI list, not
   building names.
7. **Three active construction sites** are tagged, the largest being
   **`Burlington Square Construction Site`** at `x=−202, z=−192` — the CityPlace
   block on the **west side between Cherry and Bank**. Footprints there reflect
   a site in flux and may not match the street as it stands today. Eight
   `highway=construction` ways (parts of Pine St and St Paul St) are in `roads`
   for the same reason.
8. Overpass returns the *complete* geometry of anything touching the query box,
   so the Lake Champlain multipolygon arrived hundreds of kilometres wide. All
   geometry is clipped to the bbox **plus a 60 m margin** (Sutherland–Hodgman
   for rings, Liang–Barsky for lines) so edge-straddling buildings stay whole.
   A road clipped into two pieces gets ids `w123` and `w123#1`.
9. A POI that is also street furniture — a named memorial, say — appears in
   **both** `pois` and `props`, on purpose: one is a label, the other is a
   placeable object.
